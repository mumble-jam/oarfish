# Oarfish dive: notes for Claude

A browser game in plain ES modules. No build step, no framework, no bundler. three.js r170 from a CDN via the import map in `index.html`. Read `README.md` for the file map.

## The people

Two people work on this, both through Claude. They are designers, not engineers. Explain what changed in terms of what the player sees. Keep the casual register; say plainly what is unverified.

## How to work

- Work on a branch. Do not commit to `main` or push unless asked. Branches are merged into `main` on request, after the checks pass.
- Before saying something works: run `npm test` (five suites, about ten minutes in a software renderer) and look at the result yourself with the screenshot tools. Passing checks are not the same as it looking right.
- `node tools/shot_models.mjs <prefix> [filter…]` photographs models into `out/shots/`; `node tools/shot_rift.mjs [prefix] [band…]` photographs a band's rift from nine angles, and `node tools/shot_light.mjs [prefix] [band…]` the light under its cloud at its brightest and dimmest; `python3 tools/sheet.py <out.png> <cols> <width> <files…>` tiles them into one sheet to look at.
- In this sandbox there may be no downloaded Chromium: `CHROME=/usr/bin/google-chrome npm test`.
- One control check ("holding the left button coils") depends on real-time state and fails now and then. Re-run before believing it.

## How it is built

- **Everything is flat-shaded triangles with a colour at each corner.** Models are made with `mesher()` in `game/kit.js`: a skin over cross-sections, fins of rays, eyes with pupils. A face whose colour has alpha 0 gives off its own light. Match this; do not bring in textures or imported models.
- **Appearance is a component.** `game/npc.js` holds one spec per creature or thing: palette, model, body motion, attachments, states. The game calls `make(id)`; the library calls the same. Never put a colour or a model for a creature anywhere else. Shells register from `shells.js`, plants and stonework from `world.js`, with `register()`.
- **Behaviour is not in the component.** How a biter hunts is in `foes.js`; the component only says how it looks and how its body moves.
- **World things are instanced.** `world.js` scatters white models tinted per instance. A thing's tints come from its component (`tonesOf(id)`); stone and paint come from the two colour sets (`stoneOf(band)`, `paintOf(band)`).
- **Two views of the dive.** A dive opens on the plain view: the plate, the scale bar, the key prompts, the message card, Skip tutorial and a pause button (no marker: see *Nothing points the way*). H, or the pause menu, turns on the full view (`#stage.full`, kept in `localStorage['oarfish.ui']`), which adds the key list, the tuning drawer and the model tools. Whatever a player needs in order to play has to work in the plain view; whatever is for looking things up or tuning belongs to the full one.
- **Two ways to hold the mouse** (`feel.mode`, kept in `localStorage['oarfish.feel'].mode`, so a choice survives a visit). `look`, the adventure camera, is the one a new player gets (`SHIPPED_FEEL.mode`; George asked for it as the default); it was `point` until then, and every check, shot and tool that opens the game asks for `point` itself when nothing is saved (an init script before `page.goto`), so what they measure did not move; a check in the control suite holds the default and that a saved plate camera survives a reload. `point` is the plate seen from high up: W A S D swim relative to the view; the left mouse button dragged turns the view; the right one held has the fish swim to the pointer; both held swim it forward where the view points, the mouse steering, and a view tilted off `PLATE_LEVEL` sends it down or up. A mouse is one pointer however many buttons are down, so the handlers go by `buttons`, and a second button arrives as a `pointermove`. A finger is unchanged: the fish swims to it, two fingers pinch and look. `look` is the adventure camera: the pointer is locked, the mouse turns the view with no button held, the left button wraps and the right one dashes.
- **A band's size belongs to its world, and the first band is the wide one.** The square of sea a band repeats in was one number for all five (`T = 144` at the top of `world.js`). Now a band says its own in `FLOORS` (`size`; `BASE`, 144 m, where it says nothing) and `createWorld` hands it out: `world.size`, `world.spawn` (the start, at its middle) and `world.wrap(d)` (the shortest way round). Nothing imports a size from `world.js` any more: `foes.js`, `guards.js`, `shells.js`, `boss.js` and `main.js` read it from the world they are given (inside those files it is still called `T` and `wrapD`, set at the top of each factory), and a check or a tool reads `g.world.spawn` and `g.world.size`, never a bare 72 or 144. A size has to be a multiple of 6 (pairs of the bed's 3 m cells) or `createWorld` refuses it. The first band is 204 m across, twice the area of the others, and its rift lies 100 m from the start where it lay 70 (`way`); the Keeper's ground, the mural and the nursery's road on to the rift are laid from the rift and went with it. What is scattered by chance (reefs, rocks, kelp, coral, grass, pebbles, what lives on the bed) is counted for a 144 m square and made in proportion to a band's area (`dense`), and the bed's rolls and green patches are counted the same way (`wave`), so the wide band is as thickly grown as it was and rolls as it did, not thinner and flatter. What is counted for the dive is as written: nine swarms, three puzzles, two fragments, three shells, two hooks. And the food has not moved out: swarms and hunters live in the home water, a square round the start (`home`, 144 m in the first band), because a dive lost to the Keeper begins again at the nest and has to grow again from there. So the outer ring of the first band holds natural dressing and nothing else, on purpose: it is the ground the next places are to be built on. Give anything new there a run of numbers of its own, as `xrand` is, or every world moves. (Three places now stand there: see *The quiet side*.) `node tools/shot_map.mjs <seed> <band> <out.jpg>` draws a band from straight above, to scale, with a legend (`--beside <commit>` puts the same band as it was at that commit next to it; `--nursery` draws the dive that opens in the nursery); `node tools/digest.mjs [--ref <commit>] <seeds> <bands>` prints a fingerprint of everything a band is built of, to run before and after a change that must leave a band alone. Both build the world in Node with no browser (`tools/headless.mjs`), and bands count from 0 in them as in the code. Every rift now has its mural: where the ground it is first looked for on is all taken, the search goes wider (`MURAL.wide`), and it cannot fail. Two things are left as they were found: every `h` swarm of the first band is put on the same spot in the great ring, so a second would sit on the first; and `'ring'` in the `ruins` of two bands has no builder and draws nothing (the note is at `FLOORS`). The wide band has been seen in the checks, in the plan and in a few stills; nobody has swum it.
- **The rift is the way down.** Each band but the last is a shelf cracked across the way from the start: a ragged fissure about 76 m long that steps down to a ledge and then drops sheer. It is cut into the seabed's height function in `world.js` (`rift`, `riftIn`, `riftDrop`); `world.rift` gives its frame (`nx, nz` across it, `tx, tz` along it), `in(x, z)` (metres inside its edge, negative outside), `w(u)` and `mid(u)`. Its middle is `world.goal`. Nothing stands or grows in it: placement goes through `clearNow` / `clearOfSites`, which test it. The band's keeper (`guards.js`) lives at its middle, the Keeper on the shelf at the near edge, and keeps the rift sealed until it is dealt with (`pushOut` in `guards.js`: the body is held over the ledges' height between the walls; the picture of it is the seal, below); after that, diving between the walls anywhere along it leads down. The rift is full of cloud: a bank of soft puffs lies between the walls just under the ledges, over a pale floor (`clouds` in `world.js`). The puffs are the one thing in the world that is not flat-shaded: they are round-shaded, part transparent and fade out round the rim, drawn without sorting from the lowest layer up, and as the fish sinks into it the scene's own fog closes in and takes the cloud's colour (`cam.murk` in `main.js`), so the band changes inside the cloud and the rift's bed is never seen. Under the cloud a gold light swells and sinks about every three seconds (`lamps` in `world.js`), strongest at the rift's middle, and the beam over the rift keeps time with it: it is there to be steered by. It is laid over the cloud as colour, not added as light, because added light only turns a pale cloud white.
- **The seal over a closed rift** (`seal.js`, every number in `SEAL`; held by `guards.js` as `guard.seal`). George found the old one, bars of light from wall to wall drawn additive, 'a bit rough', a web; it is gone, with `riftVeil` in `world.js`. Two looks were built in its place, switched in the testing tools (`Closed rift: glass / shoal`, `devSee.seal` in `main.js`, `guard.reseal(look)`); the first is what a player gets. Neither is a rule: `pushOut` is as it was. **Glass** (the default): a halocline, where two waters meet and the boundary looks like a pool's surface from above. One sheet over the gap between the walls, reaching `over` out onto the ledges, `lift` over their floor; a mesh a metre along by sixteen across whose corners carry where they are in the rift (`seal`: u along, k across from -1 to 1, half the width), moved in its shader: a slow swell, rings spreading from every part of the body within `touch` of it (`rips`, a ring buffer, and a dimple under each, `press`), held still at its edge. Its colour is the next band's water, darker, with slow shapes drifting in it, the gold of the light under the cloud at its middle, the sky at a slant, a wavering sheen, the sun's glints on its facets (faceted by derivatives, as everything is), and a bright line where it meets the stone; part seen through, so the cloud shows dimly. It is drawn at `renderOrder` -1, after the cloud and its lamps (-5 to -2) and before the shafts, the beam and the specks, and writes depth, so nothing of the light passes below it. Darker bands give its sheen and edge more (`shine` in `TONES`), or it would not be seen in Deep twilight. When the keeper falls it tears from the middle along its length (`tear`), opens to four fifths of its width where the tear has reached (`widen`), the two halves fold down, and it drains into the dark and is gone (`drain`, 2.8 s). **Shoal**: the rift full to the brim with 4800 small silver fish (each ten triangles, one `InstancedBufferGeometry`, one draw call, everything in the shader): each on an ellipse round the gap (its lane, from the middle to the walls) at its depth, layers turning against each other, rolling so that flashes run along it; within `near` of any of ten points of the body it is pushed away and down and closes behind. A dark sheet under it (`under`, `dim`) makes the gaps deep water and not the pale cloud. When the keeper falls they burst up and out, the middle first, and are gone in `scatter` seconds; the dark clears with them. Both read where the gap is from a table (`riftTable`: the rift's middle and the width between the walls, along it), sampled with `texelFetch`. Their tones per band are in `TONES` at the top of `seal.js`, not in `npc.js`: the shoal is the rift's lid, not a creature of the game, as the cloud is. `node tools/shot_seal.mjs <prefix> [glass|shoal] [point|look] [one bands near palace corner]` photographs it (`GAME_ROOT` for the old web). Cost, `tools/perf.mjs 0 1 2 3 --rift [--shoal]` (new: stands over each rift's middle, its keeper alive), seed 4242, against the web just before it (26b7785): the glass draws about 3,900 fewer triangles than the web did (768,009 against 771,881 in band 1) and two more draw calls (a part-clear sheet seen from both sides is drawn in two passes); the shoal about 92,000 more (863,905 in band 1, 12% more; 22 to 33% more in bands 2 to 4, which have less else to draw) and four more draw calls; the dive's own step and a drawn frame within the noise of this shared machine. Only the comparison means anything, and none of it has been on a machine with a graphics card. Seen in stills and checks only: nobody has played it, and the ripples and the shoal's turning have not been seen moving.
- **Nothing points the way.** George: 'let's get rid of a marker that's always on display. I think the player should explore everything on their own and occasionally find the crab.' The marker at the screen's edge ('the rift · 66 m', and in the nursery 'the hoop', 'the pen' and so on) is hidden from every player (`#goal` is `hidden`; `devSee.marker` in `main.js`), and shown only by `Show the marker to the rift` in the testing tools. The nursery leads by its place: a paved way from the start past every lesson to the jar and on to the rift's edge, with small hoops along the second part, and a check in the teaching suite measures that no stretch of it is bare for more than a few metres. A check or a tool that needs to know where a lesson or the rift is reads it from the dive (`g.tutTarget()`, `world.goal`, `world.rift`), never from the screen. The lines that named the marker were reworded. What is left to find a rift by outside the nursery: the pale beam over every rift and the gold light under its cloud (drawn past the fog, but from the plate's camera seen only near), the pilot fish along the way, the arcade road in band 2, the lamps in band 3, the stalls in band 4.
- **What stands on the bed stands on the bed as it is drawn.** `height(x, z)` is the bed as it is meant, and what the fish swims over. The bed that is drawn is a mesh of it: cells of 3 m, and by a rift, where a lip a metre and a half wide cannot be drawn in triangles of three, cells cut `FINE` times smaller (`fineCell`, `smallVertexY`). `drawnY(x, z)` (`world.drawn`) is the height of the triangle under a point, and everything the world builds is placed by it, so nothing hangs over a facet or sinks under one; with a radius it gives the lowest of five points, for loose things with a footprint. The rift's lip begins `RIM` inside the rift's line, a little more than the longest side of a small triangle, so that every triangle reaching outside the line is flat and the kerb, the paving and the columns at the edge stand on level ground. `tools/audit_rift.mjs [seed ...]` measures every piece within 9 m of a rift's line against the drawn bed and against the pieces under it, and lists what hangs or is buried; the game suite runs the same audit (`tools/audit_fn.mjs`) as a check.
- **The keepers do not get caught in the stonework** (`guards.js`). The Shearer goes over what stands, not round it: on its round, and on its way back from wherever a charge left it, it keeps above `world.topNear` along its own length and a little ahead (`over()`), except for a lone thing more than `HOP` taller than where it swims, which it is pushed round. A charge that runs onto anything standing 1.6 m or more (`world.inStone`: a column, a lintel, a big rock; not a kerb) stops there with a clang and leaves it spent, which also makes a column something to hide behind. Spent, it sinks no lower than the top of what is under it. If it is still getting nowhere it rises until it is (`watch.lift`). The Keeper, which walks, turns aside along whatever it is pushed back from when it has got nowhere for a while (`watch.dodge`). A check in the game suite puts each of them down in every column near its ground and between every two that stand close, and has them come home.
- **The Keeper walks on its own legs, and everything on it that works is a piece.** Its shell is one model and its eight legs and two claws are separate rigid pieces (`KEEPER`, `keeperLeg`, `keeperClaw` in `critters.js`), hung on by `crab.js` from its component's `assemble`. `legs.js` is the walker, and knows nothing about crabs: each foot stays planted while the body moves over it, steps when it is left too far behind (two groups, turn about), and comes down on `world.footing()` (the bed, or the top of a low stone). The body's height and tilt are a plane fitted through the feet, on a spring. `guards.js` only says where the crab is, which way it faces and what mood it is in; `world.steer()` keeps it out of columns. Its claws are not a pair: the right one is a big crusher and is what it strikes with, the left a small one that is never still (`KEEPER.claws`). George asked for it to be brought up to the squid's level without being rebuilt, with detail that works as tells, so `crab.js` now also hangs on: two eyes, each a stalk and an eye that turns on it (`keeperEye`); the two doors of its mouth (`keeperJaw`); four feelers, trailed by `strands.js` as one bundle (`keeperFeeler`); and a crack with a stump for each leg, shown when that leg has gone (`keeperCrack`). **The eyes are the tell for where a blow lands**: both turn together (as a real crab's do) to the fish when it is wary, and through every wind-up to the point the fight hands the rig as `act.at`, the stalks craning toward it and the eyes flaring; that point is where the claw comes down, and a check measures it. They are laid flat into their sockets when it tucks to run, loll when it is hurt, hang when it is beaten. The mouth's doors are never still and work by turns when it feeds. **A numb leg** is still paler and flickering, and now also lies low with its knee out to one side where the others arch, and jerks (the walker's foot is where it was: only how the leg is drawn changed, so what can be hit has not moved). **Damage shows**: each leg torn off leaves a stump in its socket and a red crack up the seams of the shell from it; from two legs gone (`rage`, which `guards.js` hands over only so that it can be seen) it stands higher and shakes, holds its claws up and clacks them whether or not a fish is near, and its eyes and cracks pulse. The shell has plates raised in their middles, teeth along the front of its lip, and its tail folded under it, which shows when it is on its back; the legs have a crest of spines and a pale joint before the last length; the crusher's teeth are knobs, the cutter's points, and both claws are tipped dark, as a stone crab's are. About 2050 triangles in 30 pieces, and a crack more for each leg lost (it was 680 in 21). The library shows every pose through `rig.show()`. `node tools/shot_crab.mjs [prefix]` photographs strips of it walking, of each blow of its fight, of a leg numbed and taken off, of it digging, feeding and beaten; `node tools/shot_bosses.mjs <prefix> crab` the same fight from the dive's own camera, from ahead of it, with every library pose and the model close up. A new legged creature should reuse `createWalker`, not copy it. Seen only in stills. Weak points: from behind it the eyes are hidden by the shell, so there the tell is only the claw and the mark on the bed; under the sand none of this shows; and the numb leg's knee can pass through the leg next to it.
- **What is drawn is what hits** (`shapes.js`; George played the Keeper and was 'almost certain that the hit model and the actual model are not quite the same', and they were not: its blows hit discs, its legs were judged from above, the fish was a row of points and nothing about the crab was solid). Everything the Keeper's fight judges by is one of three shapes in the world's space: a capsule, a capsule's footprint stood up off the bed (`prism`), and an ellipsoid. The fish is a capsule along each length of spine, as thick as `oarfish.js` draws the body there (`fishBody`, `fishRadius`: about 0.15 m each side at the neck, tapering), and the head one more, to the tip of the snout. The crab's shell is an ellipsoid and each length of each leg a capsule, read off the pieces as they are drawn this frame (`rig.solids()` in `crab.js`, so a numb leg is where it lies). They are solid: `rig.js` hands the snout, the trail the body follows and each joint as the chain is built to `arena.bend`, and the Keeper turns each out of them (`bend`, `pushOut` in `guards.js`); the trail is kept, so a body pushed to one side of a leg stays there. The blows are judged after the crab has been drawn for the frame (`S.pending`, the end of `update`): the great claw by its hand and fingers as they land (`rig.landing` before, `rig.strike` at the moment, which puts the claw there at once), stood up from the bed to `slam.high`, and a jolt round its fingertips (`slam.shock`); a jab by the small claw likewise; a sweep by the great claw wherever it is drawn each frame of it; a run by the shell and the legs; its rising from the sand by its shell's rim and `dig.burst`. A blow takes the part of the body nearest the tail among those it touches (`struck`). A leg is hurt by the tip of the snout coming within `leg.reach` of its skin, in three dimensions; the shell turns it. The marks on the bed are the footprints, flat stadiums, not round glows. To make the shapes honest the model was changed rather than the threat shrunk, as George's rule had it: the great claw is a third bigger, the small one reaches as far as its jab, the legs are longer (knees about two metres up), the sweep goes across at a fish's height. **Show hit shapes** in the pause menu's testing tools draws what the fight believes over the picture (`createShapeView`, `guard.shapes()`): blue the fish, white what is solid, green a leg that can be hurt now and grey one that cannot, amber a blow on its way, red what a blow was judged by as it landed. `node tools/shot_shapes.mjs <prefix>` photographs it blow by blow from the dive's camera and from straight above. The hit rates were measured with a scripted fish that dashes off at a random moment of each wind-up (`node tools/rate_keeper.mjs`, sixty tries a blow, so take a few hundredths either way as noise): before and after, the slam took a body about 0.67 and 0.60 of the time, the sweep 0.54 and 0.68, the jab 0.95 and 0.82, the run 0.52 and about 0.45, the dig 0.68 and 0.58; a ram aimed at a leg landed 0.88 and about 0.65 of the time. Weak points: the shapes of the other three keepers are as they were; and the numbers above are for the Keeper before it stood tall (on its own, with knees about two metres up, a fish at its lowest only just reached the front knees while it was stuck), so take them again with `rate_keeper.mjs` before tuning the tall one.
- **The Keeper is a fight to be read** (`KEEPER_FIGHT` and the `keeper` branch of `update` in `guards.js`; George asked for a Souls boss). It takes up with any part of a body within 18 m, on its own ground (a leash from the middle of it, and never out over the rift), and goes for the part nearest it. Every blow has a wind-up with its own look (`act` in `crab.js`), lands where it was aimed and not where the fish has got to, takes the part of the body nearest the tail that is still in its way (`struckAt`: a dash carries the head clear, not what trails), and leaves it open. The great claw: up over its head for 0.95 s, following the body for the first six tenths and then aimed, with a mark on the bed; three segments, and then the claw is stuck for 2.4 s. A sweep of the same claw, when a body lies across its front: two segments, and it passes under a body that has risen. A jab of the small claw at what is near on its left: one. A run, sideways, at a body farther off: two, and a column stops it. A shed tail within 8 m is a parry: it leaves any wind-up and goes to eat, open all the while; one lying where it cannot get to it (past its leash or over the rift, `bait.near`), or that it has tried to reach for `bait.most` seconds, it gives up on and does not go for again. Its shell turns a ram (`ramKeeper`); what can be hurt is a leg (`legAt`, against the leg as drawn), and only while it is open (`S.open`: stuck, resting after a blow, reeling, feeding). A ram cracks a leg, a second numbs it (it lies low, pale and flickering, 4.5 s, and then is cracked still), and a third, or a body drawn round the numb leg (`guard.ring`, `wrapped`), takes it off (see the tall Keeper, below, for the rest of what its legs do), and it lies where it fell (`rig.shed`; `legs.js` walks on with a leg `lame` or `gone`). Two legs gone and it rages: quicker, and it digs (down, under the sand toward the body's middle with silt showing where, and up under it for three). Four gone and it is done. A full crest breaks its guard instead of bouncing, and on an open leg takes it off in one. Three lines teach it, each once. All the numbers are in `KEEPER_FIGHT`; none has been played by a person yet. The other three keepers are as they were.
- **The Keeper stands tall, and its legs are the fight** (George, after playing it: he wanted 'a pretty epic battle where I have to go by his legs, and his legs should be fun and meaningful to engage with'). The model: drawn `KEEPER.scale` (1.3) times its modelled size and carried `ride` high, so its belly is about three metres over the bed, over a fish at its lowest (`rig.js` holds the head 1.5 m off the bed), its knees four and a half to five, and its feet ten to eleven metres apart (`rest`); a fish swims in under it and out between its legs. Each leg is three lengths, the last a thick spiked tip that stands straight down (`l3`, `last` in `legs.js` and `keeperLeg`); the claws are longer to reach the bed from up there. The look is a spider crab's (the Japanese spider crab carries a body the size of a table on legs that span three and a half metres) with the stone crab's great claw. `legs.js` also lets the fight take a leg over (`ctrl`): its foot goes where it is told and carries no weight. The rules, every number in `KEEPER_FIGHT`: (1) a leg goes whole, cracked (its seams glow red), numb (pale, flickering, lying low), off (a stump); a ram moves it on one, and a numb leg comes back to cracked. (2) A leg with its weight on it rings off a ram unless the Keeper is open; one caught lifted (mid-step, or raised to stomp) is knocked away, cracked, and the Keeper stumbles, open (`leg.stumble`). (3) A body under it is out of the great claw's reach: it stomps, a leg up high and flaring (the tell, `stomp.tell` 0.8 s, its spot marked on the bed), then down where the body was, for two; the spike sticks for `stomp.stuck` and that leg takes a ram double, open or not (`stuckFoot`). (4) A body that lingers under it `belly.linger` seconds: it heaves up on its toes, shuddering, silt raining from its belly (0.9 s), and drops its weight, for three, then lies on its belly, open. (5) A body wound round any leg holds it (`S.held`, the part of the body that went round it keeps it): swum away, it drags the foot out, and at `haul.topple` metres the Keeper goes over, open, the leg numb; held `haul.shed` seconds and not hauled, it sheds the leg itself, which counts as a leg gone. (6) A leg that has come off lies where it fell (`limbs`): it is bait the Keeper goes to eat, and a fish passing over it eats it for `shed.food` fish (`game.fed` in `main.js`). (7) With two of its four right-hand legs gone (its great claw's side) it lists that way and the great claw is 1.35 times slower; with two of its four hind legs gone it neither runs nor digs; it turns slower toward the side that has lost more. (8) The wave, after the fiddler crab's (a male's claw wave quickens as a rival nears, and they wrestle only if the bluff fails): the great claw waves at a fish within 18 m, slowly far off and quicker as it nears, frantic in the last `wave.warn` seconds before it commits to a blow, and the first time a fish comes near it waves a while (`wave.bluff`) before its first blow. Still four legs to win, and it rages at two. The camera stands back `view` metres while a fish is near it (`guard.view`, read by `main.js`). `node tools/shot_tall.mjs <prefix>` photographs all of it (with `OARFISH_SRC` and `size`, the crab as it was, at the same scale). Seen only in stills and in the checks. Weak points: the wave's tempo cannot be seen in a still and has not been seen moving; a leg raised to stomp from the far side is hidden by the great claw from some angles; the numb leg's knee still passes through its neighbour; the walker's legs can cross when it turns hard; and a whole fight with every rule in it has not been played by anyone.
- **The Shearer bends, and is made of pieces.** George asked for it to be redone to the squid's level, with what hangs, trails or works as pieces that move by themselves. Its head, wings and trunk are one model (`shearer`, `SHEARER` in `critters.js`) whose head turns and nods on a neck and whose body lies along its turn, all in its shader (`bendy` in `spine.js`); `sawfish.js` poses it from where it is, how it really moved and what mood it is in, and hangs the rest on it. The saw is rigid (`shearerSaw`): the blade, the end beyond where it would break, and thirty-two teeth, each a piece of its own, carried on the head wherever the neck has turned it (the rig does the shader's sum again for that). The tail, from behind the hind fins, is a strand (`shearerTail`, `strands.js`): it is told the sweep it would make in still water and goes after it late, so it swings wide when the body turns and hangs when the fish is spent. - **The Shearer is a fight of lines and stillness** (`SHEARER_FIGHT` and the `shearer` branch of `update` in `guards.js`; the second boss made over after the Keeper, at George's word). It feels the water a fish moves. Winding up, its line is drawn on the water (`lineTo`): it follows a tail that is moving, aimed ahead of it (`tailV`), and slides off one that is still (`Z.still`, `Z.aim.slide`), until at seven tenths of the wind-up it locks and turns bright. - **A keeper's instrument can take the floor** (`SHOW`, `playShow`, `flourish` in `music.js`). - **A line turns round every few bars** (`TURN`, `TURN_RHYTHM`, `turnNotes` in `music.js`; George: the Keeper's tuba 'pam-pam' should go 'pam-pam, pam-pam, pam-pam, pam-pa-PAM-pam'). One rule for any repeating line, not a crab special: on a turnaround bar (always the bar before a chord change) the second half of the bar leads into the next bar's first note, which is not touched, and bars that are not turnaround bars are played exactly as before. Four music sliders in the drawer, in presets like the rest (`turn`, `turnsize`, `turnheight`, `turnall`; shipped values in `TURN_SHIPPED` in `main.js`, and a code from before them gives those): **Turnaround** off, every 8, 4 (shipped) or 2 bars; **size**, one short passing note before beat three (the Keeper: root, the scale note nearest halfway, fifth) up to a run of four, shipped a walk-up of three (`pam` root, `pa` on the swung eighth before beat three, `PAM` on beat three a touch louder, 1.12, `pam` on beat four, then the next bar); **height**, how many steps of the scale under its landing note a walk-up starts, from as close as the notes allow up to seven, never under the line's `low` (shipped 20: stepwise). The notes are the scale of the chord the band is on in that bar (`chordAt`), the last a step under the note the line plays on the next chord (`nextChord` also guesses the mood the band picks every eight bars). Every keeper's line turns round; the ordinary creatures' only with **Creatures turn round too** (off as shipped), each on its own odd bar of the interval (`slot`), and only one line in a bar, a keeper first (`st.turnBy`). A keeper's fill or solo always wins: nothing turns round in a bar where a show sounds or rings into (`st.showEnd`). The library's Hear it plays four bars ending on the turnaround and the note it lands on. `music.line(id, bars)` plays one line alone and `st.log` writes down every note the cast and the shows ask for, with its bar and tick; `node tools/hear_turn.mjs [--ref <commit>]` renders a line alone and with the band to WAV in `out/sound/` with no sound card, writes its notes as a table, and checks that with the turnaround off the band is sample for sample what `--ref` renders. - **Squid: the rival that answers pressure in steps** (`BLOOM.squid`). The shoal rests at home, heading into the current; while the fish is within 50 m it goes raiding every twenty seconds, by turns to the fry and to the nearest other swarm within 46 m (never the great swarm or the first one), rings it, and strikes one fish every 2.6 s (`aim` pose, then `strike`, a spark where the fish was), six to a raid. Each squid has a fright that a fish raises by closing on it faster than 0.9 m/s within 10 m, and by being nearer than 1.7 m, and that cools when it does not (faster when the fish is all but still). At 0.3 it bluffs (the `bluff` pose: facing the fish, giving ground); at 0.65 it goes pale and jets 9 m off, alone; at 1, or at once for a dash within 6 m or a body drawn round it, it leaves a copy of itself in ink and the whole shoal scatters, each its own way, gathering at home again after 14 s. A dash through an ink copy within its first 5 s (`hold` + `fade`, as before) bursts it. **The ink is liquid in liquid** (`ink.js`, every number in `BLOOM.ink`; George saw the first copy, the mantle drawn solid in dark purple with a halo, and said it looked 'like a dark solid object, not as a liquid in liquid'). It is a heap of soft puffs in the same family as the silt and the rift's cloud: low round shapes of one flat colour, see-through, each thinning to nothing toward its rim, writing no depth and casting no shadow from the sun. As it is left the puffs lie along the squid as it was posed (a slice of the mantle and fins to a puff, the head, three long puffs down each arm), so for a beat it is a dense copy with the squid's outline; then each puff swells and thins, rounds out, and is carried by a slow curling water (an ABC flow), sinking a little and drifting with the current; seven unseen leaders go off from the arm tips and the mantle's point and leave thin puffs behind them, which are the wisps and tendrils. A part of the fish passing through pushes the puffs aside and along, tearing a hole, and a quick one drags streamers out after it; a dash through it blows it apart and it thins away sooner. A soft faint stain lies on the bed under it. It is gone at `life` (13.4 s), before the shoal gathers, thinning to nothing with no pop. Seen in stills and checks only: nobody has swum through it. Weak points: the squid it copies is still there for the first fraction of a second, so as it is left the copy is half hidden behind the pale squid jetting off; the wisps can look like flecks from above; and the puffs are not sorted, which is right only because they are all nearly one colour. A jump in where the fish is (the testing tools put it down) frightens nothing. Instrument: pizzicato.
- **The squid's mini-game: settle the shoal** (`BLOOM.settle`; the report's "gently" candidate). No words: come within 10 m of the shoal and a try starts; hang within 3.2 m of a squid, slower than 1.3 m/s, with every squid's fright under 0.2, for 2.5 s, and the shoal lets the fish stay. The meter is the shoal: bluffs say too fast, and as the hold builds they turn one by one to face the fish. A try is lost to ink, or after 60 s (they go hunting), and starts again on coming back. Graded by time: under 45 s one, 25 s two, 14 s three, and four for the third done without a single bluff or flight. It pays in the dive: a settled shoal raids no more that dive. - **The kelp forest's life** (`kelpwood.js`, every number in `KELPWOOD` at its top; the research report's sections 5, 7 and 8). One **box jelly** (the sea wasp, *Chironex fleckeri*) keeps the clearing at the far end, drawn 2.4 times its model (a bell of about 0.65 m, tentacles of about 3 m). True of the animal: it hunts actively by day, it swam away from dark objects in the laboratory, and its sting is counted by how much tentacle touches skin. - **Garden eels: the meadow sinks ahead of you** (`SANDFLAT.eels`). Thirteen colonies of twenty fill the cistern rim (hex rings 3.3 m apart); their own patches of sand are not drawn, and the 260 holes are one instanced piece. An eel goes all the way down when any part of the body is nearer (in 3D, from 0.3 m over its hole) than `calm` 0.9 m plus 0.7 m for every m/s of the body's noise over 1.6 m/s; noise is the speed, plus 0.12 times how hard it speeds up or slows down, and 13 while dashing. So: 0.9 m at the slowest swim (the body is held 1.5 m off the bed, so nothing goes down), about 2.1 m at a plain swim (the divers' figure in Yunger et al.), 4.3 m at a sprint, 9 m dashing; between that and 1.2 m more it is part way down. A crossing is the head in over the rim and out at least 1.1 rim-radii from where it came in; graded by the part of the eels the body passed within 2.5 m of that never went all the way down (0.6 one, 0.85 two, every one three; fewer than twelve passed, no grade); a note for each step, and the best is drawn in the nest (below). Measured with a scripted fish on the same line: a plain swim passes about 120 eels and sends 73 down (no grade); the slowest swim sends none down (three), while the nearest still sink to about a quarter out.
- **Hermit crabs: a shell market** (`SANDFLAT.market`). That shell comes off the body (`hooks.setDown`), falls to the head of the line, and the chain runs, about six seconds: each crab feels the shell ahead, leaves its own, turns, backs into the new one and turns back, and the shell it left is handed to the next (each rig owns its shell pieces, so a shell is laid again in the next crab's rig where it lay). The two sites are used by turns, which the real fish never does.

- **What it costs** (`tools/perf.mjs 0`, seed 4242, against 26b7785): standing in the sand flat 717 k to about 838 k triangles (17% more), 340 to about 478 draw calls (four crabs of about thirty pieces each are most of it), a drawn frame 151 to about 207 ms in this software renderer; at the start nothing to speak of. Only the comparison means anything here. To cut it further: the crabs' pieces, or fewer eel colonies.
- **New creatures follow `CREATURES.md`**: the real animal first, one trait, a body the world moves, a pose for every mood, and pictures before it is called done. `node tools/shot_poses.mjs <prefix> <id>` photographs every pose any creature has.
- **The world answers the fish, and nothing depends on it.** All of it is in `stir()` in `world.js`, run at the end of `update()`, and none of it is a rule: no food, no harm, nothing on the HUD. (1) What grows: `kit.growing` takes `give` (it leans out of the fish's way: kelp, grass, sea fans, lantern flowers) or `shy` (it pulls in: anemone, clam, sea lily, tube worms, sea pen). `shy` moves only what the model marked with `m.tuck(home)` in the mesher, so the mark and the option go together; a model without marks must not be given `shy`. The plants read `wake`: twelve points kept along the body and, fading over 2.5 s, where the tail has been, with a circle (`wake.bound`) outside which no plant does the sum. (2) Silt: puffs under any part of the body moving within about 1.9 m of the bed. The fish is held 1.5 m off the bed by `rig.js`, so that is as close as it gets: only a fish kept down trails silt. (3) Pilot fish (`pilot` in `npc.js`): five to a band, waiting along the way to the well; they ride beside the body, keep up with a swim and not a sprint, and wait where they are left. They are not a swarm and cannot be eaten. (4) Chimes: bars hung under lintels and on lines between ring columns (`hang`, `strung`), never at the rift. A bar touched by the body swings aside and is pushed onto `world.react.rang`; `main.js` hands each to `music.chime`, which takes the note from the chord the band is on at that moment. Chimes and pilot fish are placed from a third run of numbers (`xrand`), so adding or moving them never shifts the layout or the dressing. `node tools/shot_react.mjs` photographs all of it.
- **A catch is a small show, in this order** (`main.js`): the loop closes and the dive holds still for an instant (`run.freeze`, longer for a bigger swarm; it is honoured by `tick`, not by a forced `step`); the band hits as hard as the catch was big (`music.hit('catch', size)`); the swarm streams to the mouth nearest first (`gulp`), each arrival stepping a run of notes up the scale (`music.gulp`); and only when the last is in does the fish grow (`addFood`), with a swell of light down its body (`growPulse`, drawn by the fish's own materials from `fishPulse`). So for about a second after a catch the food is in the water, not in `run.food`: a check that reads length straight after a catch must wait for the stream. Catches in a row are a chain (`CHAIN`, `run.chain`): one within nine seconds of the last adds a layer to the band (vibraphone, then horns, then a saxophone solo: `playChain` in `music.js`), a bite or a lapse ends it. Length shows on the fish: `stature(segs)` in `oarfish.js` is 0 to 3, a step at 14, 19 and 24 segments, which are the lengths that open the next size of swarm; at each the crest is built taller (`CREST`) and the fins glow deeper (`glowFish`). Reaching one plays `crest` instead of `grow`.
- **The oars are a first version, built to be argued with.** George asked for a way of travelling that is easy to learn and hard to master, where keys pressed in some combination add up to a harmony. What was built (`OARS`, `stroke`, `rowing` in `main.js`; `pulse`, `oar`, `hum` in `music.js`; `rig.tempo` and `rig.pull` in `rig.js`): two oars (J and K, or 1 and 2; Cross and Circle on a controller) pulled in turn on the band's beat. A pull in time adds a voice to a chord the band holds for the fish, up to five, and each voice is 15% more speed; two beats unrowed and they start to go. Mastery: a pull on the swung off-beat adds drive on top (5% each, up to eight); both oars together on the first beat of a bar, with three voices or more, is a surge; a pull out of time, or the same oar twice, costs a voice. The beat is the band's own (`music.pulse()` reads the audio clock, less the output's delay) and falls back to a plain count from the dive's clock when the band is not playing, which is also how the checks row (`oars.metronome`). It shows as two lights at the head and a ring at every pull; there is nothing on the HUD. Not yet played by a person: the windows, the keys and whether it is fun are all open.
- **Body puzzles** (`PUZZLE`, `puzzles`, `solve()` in `world.js`): three kinds in every band but the last, which has two, each opening a cache, all optional and with no words. A capstan (a post headed with the carved coil: go round it one and a quarter times and its line draws the lid off a cist; the wrap keys circle it as they do a swarm, through `world.react.posts`), a row of four windows (the nursery's hoops: thread all four in one pass, six seconds allowed between two), and a vent (a chimney breathing silt and its capped twin: a body lying over the open one for a second and a half blows the cap off the other, and that cache also leaves a shell). Each shows how far along it is by what moves, and undoes itself if left half done. An opened cache is pushed onto `world.react.opened`; `openCache` in `main.js` streams its food to the mouth and counts it in `run.caches[band]`. They are built only from pieces the ruins already had, are placed last from `xrand`, and mark their ground in `sites` with a fourth element so that no jar is put there. `node tools/shot_puzzles.mjs <prefix> <band>` photographs them.
- **Hauling** (`HAUL`, `hauling()` in `main.js`; `fragments` in `world.js`): two fallen column tops lie in every band, a good way from the rift. Going round one as round a swarm takes it up (`run.haul`); it rides under the middle of the body, which is then tied up: no wrapping or coiling, a swim at 85%, and the shed key lets the load go in place of shedding the tail. A bite shakes it loose. One that has been let go lies for `HAUL.wait` seconds before it can be taken up again, so a body still curled round it does not snatch it straight back. Carried out past the rift's ledge, or to the palace gate, it is given to the deep: counted in `run.hauled` for the end card and in `localStorage['oarfish.nest'].fragments` for the nest, where each one given raises one of the shells on show. `tools/shot_haul.mjs` photographs it lying, carried and given.
- **Swarms have tempers** (`TEMPER` in `npc.js`; `bolting`, `twinning`, `seeking` in `world.js`). Each band's `kinds` in `FLOORS` gives one letter to a swarm of its `schools`, so which swarm has which temper never depends on the seed. `b` bolts: cornered by a head that is not at least `over` metres above it, it climbs out of the plane the body turns in; with the head over it, it holds. `t` parts in two when cornered, each half a swarm one size down (the other half is a second entry at the end of `world.schools`, with `of` pointing back, empty until the parting); left alone for `mend` seconds the halves join again. `g` will not bunch away from a lantern flower: with the head near it runs, bearing for the nearest one, and bunches in its light. The loose shape tells them apart before anything happens: a column, two lobes, a thin wide sheet (`slack`, `tall`, `gap` read by `place()` in `npc.js`, and shown as states of every swarm in the library). `world.react.swarms` carries what one has just done to `main.js`, which gives it a sound and, the first time, a line. The great swarm, the swarms of the nursery and any swarm in a pen that would need room are always the plain sort. `tools/shot_swarms.mjs` photographs all three.
- **Cleaning stations** (`CLEAN`, `stations`, `tend()` in `world.js`; the `circle` state and `startle()` in `foes.js`; the answer to `world.react.cleaned` in `main.js`): one in every band that has tail-biters, a coral head with six cleaner wrasse over it and a pale ring that fills as they work. The whole body held still inside the ring (every joint from the snout to the tail, the head slower than `still`) for `time` seconds has one segment grown back (George, after seeing a fish with only its head in: 'the longer the oarfish is, the harder it is to accomplish the task'). The ring is `reach` (3.5 m, was 3.2) for the starting body and widens `grow` (0.3 m) for every metre of body past `from` (`cleanReach`), a third of what the body grows: a long body has to curl up to fit, and a resting fish still creeps forward at rig.js's idle 0.22 m/s, so the ring has to hold the curl and the creep (measured with a scripted fish that coils once round the middle and lets go: the farthest any part got in the 7.5 s after it was still was 3.3 m at 10 segments, 4.6 m at 26; the ring is 3.5 and 5.0). The ring teaches it with no words: a faint whole circle, a bright arc over it as far round as the share of the body inside (`bodyIn`), warm points on every part still outside (`bodyMarks`, one draw call), and the white arc of the work just inside it; the line says 'Curl your whole body inside its ring and hold still'. Weak point: the rings are one-pixel lines, as every ring in the game is, so from the plate camera drawn back they are faint; the warm points on the body carry it there; a station does it `uses` times in a dive with `rest` seconds between, and then its cleaners go in for good. While the work goes on, `ctx.truce` is set and no tail-biter within `call` metres bites: up to `most` of them come and go round, closing from 7.5 m to 3.4 m as the work goes, and the others keep to their own water. When it is done they start back from the body (`start` seconds) and it is `grace` seconds before any hunts again; broken off, there is the grace but no starting back, and nothing is given. Two of each band's tail-biters have their homes 15 m from the station (`foes.js`), so the promise is kept in every band. Measured with a scripted fish: leaving at a sprint as soon as it is done keeps the segment about seven times in eight; a plain swim, five in eight; waiting four seconds, never. `tools/shot_clean.mjs` photographs it.
- **The mural at each rift** (`MURAL`, `mural`, `SIGNS`, `carve()`, `earn()` in `world.js`): a stele beside the way in, a little back from the rift's near lip and off the Keeper's ground in the first band, with three carvings and no words. `main.js` calls `world.react.earn('swarm' | 'cache' | 'guard')` when the band's greatest swarm is taken whole (`world.react.greatest`: the biggest size in the band's `schools`), a cache is opened, or the keeper is beaten. A carving that has been won lights only when the head comes within `near` metres, one after another, each with a note; all three lit, the painted top lights and the band answers. The third carving is the keeper's own sign (claw, saw, lamp, jaws). It is this dive's doing, in this band: nothing on it is kept. The palace, which has no rift, has none. `tools/shot_mural.mjs` photographs it.
- **The nest** (`NEST` and the block that builds `nestAt` in `world.js`; `nest`, `remember()` and `nestShells` in `main.js`): where every dive starts, in the first band only, and not on the dive that opens in the nursery. `localStorage['oarfish.nest']` holds `{ record, shells, fragments }`: the longest body there has been, in segments; the kinds of shell found; the column tops given to rifts. `remember()` writes it as it happens (a segment grown past the record, a shell taken up), so a dive cut short still counts. `main.js` hands it to `createWorld` as `nest`, and the world lays, from the start point and the start heading: to the left a line of stones as long as the record body, painted at both ends; to the right ten places for shells in two rows, a dish each. The world makes the places and `main.js` hangs the shells over them (`world.react.nest.places`, `.group`), since the shell kit is its own. The first of the found shells stand on hauled column tops, one for each fragment given. Nothing in the nest does anything: it is a record. `tools/shot_nest.mjs [prefix] [segments] [shells] [fragments]` photographs it for any record.
- **Unlocks** (`SHELL_FEATS`, `shellPool()` in `shells.js`; `feat()` and `run.pool` in `main.js`): four kinds of shell are not to be found until a feat has been done once, in any dive: the Spine for a band's greatest swarm taken, the Tusk for a cache opened, the Horn for a keeper beaten, the Pearl for a whole mural lit. They are the feats the murals count, and `feat()` is called beside each `earn()`. A feat is kept in `localStorage['oarfish.nest'].feats`, said once in a toast, and changes nothing in the dive it happens in: `run.pool` is fixed when a dive starts, and everything that hands out a shell draws from it (the shells lying about each band, a cache's prize, a keeper's drop). In the nest the place of each of the four has the sign of its feat lying in the dish, dark until it is done and lit ever after. Two testing tools in the pause menu: `Unlock every shell` and `Empty the nest`.
- **Nothing is drawn twice that has not moved, and nothing casts that the sun's box does not hold.** (1) While the dive is paused, `main.js` draws a frame only when something is pressed, clicked, changed or resized (`stir()`), and once a second in case; an end card nobody has touched for `QUIET.away` seconds goes the same way. Anything new that changes the picture while paused without an event has to call `stir()`. The library, which turns its specimen, is always drawn. (2) Every kind of dressing that casts has a second, shadow-only mesh (`Scatter.shade` in `world.js`) holding just the pieces inside the sun's shadow box, seated again each time the picture's centre has gone `SIGHT.step` metres (`dress()`); the mesh that is drawn casts nothing and keeps its pieces in the order they were made, because two flags in one plane show whichever is drawn last. The box is read off the sun's own shadow camera, so changing it in `main.js` needs nothing here. A check draws nine views with and without and wants them the same to the pixel; `world.sorting(false)` has everything cast and `world.tally()` counts. **Do not leave out of the picture what is past the fog**: it was tried, and far columns show as dark shapes against the sea's surface and the shafts of light, which the fog does not touch.
- **A lighter picture is asked for, never given** (`light`, `setLight` in `main.js`; the "Lighter picture" box in the pause menu, kept in `localStorage['oarfish.feel'].light`, off for a new player). On, there is one pixel to a point of the screen and not two, the sun's shadow map is half the size, and a frame arriving less than `light.gap` milliseconds after the last is skipped, so a screen of 120 or 144 Hz is drawn at 60 or 72. George's fan was the reason; it has not been measured on his machine.
- **A dive left alone swims figures** (`adrift`, `figures()`, `FIGURES` in `main.js`). George did not want it to pause itself. After `QUIET.away` seconds with nothing pressed, held or moved, in the middle of a dive, the fish is steered by `figures()` and not by the player: a ring to the left and one to the right, a ring that climbs and comes down, a coil, a long wave, round and round, never more than a few metres from where it was left. Nothing bites it meanwhile, and any touch gives it back with a moment's grace: that is `run.hush`, which `game.safe()` honours and which, unlike `run.safe`, does not make the fish blink (George did not like it blinking the whole time). A page a test is driving (`navigator.webdriver`) is never left to itself unless the test sets `adrift.allow`.
- **Length is the dive's money**, and its prices are in one place: `LENGTH` at the top of `main.js`. Growing a segment costs more the longer the fish is (`growCost`: 5 fish at the start, 17 at the top, 170 for the whole climb). Each rift takes a toll of tail segments (`riftToll`: four tenths of what is over eight, at least two), marked dark on the scale bar while the fish is over an open rift; shells on the taken tail move up the body. A fish that cannot spare the toll and still keep eight segments is turned back while there is a swarm in the band it could take, and let through for nothing when there is not, so nobody is trapped. Full grown, food fills the crest (`run.charge`, the glow over the head): charged, a dash goes half as far again and the next ram that lands counts double, stuns what is near, and spends it. The nursery depends on the first four segments costing 24 fish or fewer.
- **A DualSense does not vibrate through the browser on Windows.** Chrome and Edge read its buttons but only have motor code for the DualShock 4 (`device/gamepad/raw_input_gamepad_device_win.cc` in Chromium), so `gamepad.vibrationActuator` is null whatever the cable. `pad.js` therefore has a second way: “Connect DualSense motors” in the pause menu asks the browser for the controller (WebHID, Chrome and Edge only, not inside an embedded frame) and sends Sony's output report itself, laid out as in the Linux driver `hid-playstation.c`. The browser remembers the pick, and `pad.direct.resume()` takes it up on later visits. The motors run until told to stop, so every `play` arms a stop. This has been checked against the layout and a stand-in device only, never a real controller from here.
- **Library edits are an overlay.** `tweaks` in `npc.js` stores colour and size changes in `localStorage['oarfish.npc']`; `resolve(id)` lays them over the defaults. To make a pasted "Oarfish NPC changes" note permanent, write the values into the component's `palette` (or its scale) in the code.

## Things that break quietly

- **Two streams of chance in `world.js`.** `rand` decides where everything that matters stands; `drand` is for dressing only. Adding a `rand()` call, or changing how many a builder makes, moves everything in every world. Players never see the number a world is built from, so that costs them nothing, but the checks stand in world 4242 (`startRun(4242)`) and some of them lean on where things are there. Use `drand`/`dr`/`dpick` for anything decorative, and expect to revisit a check or two when the layout really has to change. To know whether a change has moved a band, do not guess: `node tools/digest.mjs --ref <the commit before> <seeds> <bands>` and the same without `--ref` must print the same lines.
- **Tints lists keep their length.** `pick(tones)` draws from `rand`; a list of a different length shifts the stream.
- **The nursery** (the tutorial opening) rebuilds the start of band 1. The checks turn it off with `window.__noNursery = true`; `tools/test_teach.mjs` is the one suite that runs it.
- **Browser features need a click and may be refused**: pointer lock, full screen, clipboard, audio. Every call is in a click handler and catches refusal. Storage is wrapped in try/catch and may come back empty.
- **Feedback is copied, never sent.** "Leave feedback" (pause menu, and the end of a dive) opens a sheet in the pause menu's place (`#pause.noting`, `openNote` in `main.js`). The note, with a few lines on how the dive went that the player can read and leave out, goes to the clipboard or to the device's share sheet for them to send themselves. The site has no server and collects nothing; keep it that way unless the owner asks for a form or an address to send to.
- **Testing tools** are a section of the pause menu (`#pause .dev`), shown in either view: go straight to any band, grow four segments, beat the band's keeper, show the old marker to the rift, and switch the closed rift's look (glass, shoal). Under `Bring one to look at` there is a button for every detailed creature, the ones marked `bring` in `npc.js` (the squid and the four jellyfish, in the dive or not, and the sand flat's six): it puts that creature in the water beside the fish so the model can be seen where it would live, and pressed again takes it away (`bring`, `pets`, `squidStep`, `jellyStep`, `LIVES` in `main.js`). The sand flat's creatures live by `sandpets.js` instead (`SAND_PETS`, `bringSand`: each a pet with `step(dt)` and `dispose()`, which `bring` and `petStep` hand over to): a line of four hermit crabs by an empty shell that go in as the fish comes near, a trumpetfish that hangs head down by a still fish and lies along a swimming one, the goby and shrimp at their hole (they flick, and go down, by how near the body is), a colony of garden eels that sink near the body, the puffer ploughing its finished circle, and three circles at three stages. The squid hovers and jets about. A jellyfish simply lives: it beats, each beat really moves it, it sinks a little between beats, wanders, and is there again beside the fish if left far behind; the moon jellies come six together. None of it is a rule and nothing is harmed. A new component marked `bring` gets its button by itself, but needs an entry in `LIVES` or `SAND_PETS` (or it is treated as the squid). `Go to the bloom water` puts the fish at the bloom's edge, on the side toward the start, facing in. `Go to the sand flat`, `Go to the reef head` and `Go to the kelp forest` put it 7 m outside that place's arch, facing in (see *The quiet side*). `Show hit shapes` draws over the picture what the Keeper's fight judges by (see *What is drawn is what hits*). `Empty the nest` forgets the squid's best too, and the box jelly's, the sand flat's records and the reef's coral. Add to it when something new needs a shortcut to be tested by hand. (They were first hidden in the plain view, and George could not find them.)
- **The frame gauge** (`gauge.js`, every number in `GAUGE`; two buttons in the testing tools; made so George can measure on his own laptop, since every cost above was taken here with no graphics card). `Show frame time` (off on every load) puts a builder's readout in the top-left corner, updated twice a second: frame time (the average over the last second, from one drawn frame to the next, so it holds the graphics card's time and cannot be quicker than the screen's refresh; and the worst in the last five), frames a second, the last frame's draw calls and triangles from `renderer.info`, and the frame's work outside drawing (the dive's step). `Measure the first band` tours the start, the bloom water, the sand flat, the reef head, the middle of the kelp forest's main lane and 13 m off the Keeper on the start's side, from the adventure camera and then the plate's: each placed as the "Go to…" tools place, 1 s to settle and 4 s measured while the fish swims gently on (`run.hush`, not `run.safe`, which would make it blink), then a plain table (graphics card, browser, pixels, pixel ratio, Lighter picture) shown and put on the clipboard; any key or click stops it; the band, place, body, camera and pause are put back (a player in another band gets that band again fresh, and what the tour stirred in the first band, a frightened squid and so on, stays stirred). `main.js` makes it in one block just before `renderer.setAnimationLoop`, which it wraps; only while a tool is on does it also wrap `renderer.render`, so with both off nothing is added to the page. The checks reach it as `window.__gauge`; `node tools/measure_band.mjs [seed] [--quick]` runs the tour in headless Chrome and prints the table (here only its shape means anything).
- **`window.__game`** is the hook the checks drive. `sim(seconds, forced)` advances the dive without real time. Keep it working when you change `main.js`.

## Words on screen

Short, concrete, no jargon. A lesson is shown by the place first, a key prompt second, a sentence last. Messages go through `tell()` / `say()` and appear in the card over the scale bar; give them a kind (`teach`, `warn`, `shell`, `note`).
